Cyber Fraud

What is Cyber Fraud?

Cyber fraud is a type of cybercrime in which criminals use computers, mobile devices, or the internet to deceive individuals or organizations for financial gain or to steal confidential information. Fraudsters often impersonate trusted organizations or create fake websites, emails, and messages to trick victims into revealing sensitive data.

Types of Cyber Fraud

  • Phishing: Fake emails or websites used to steal usernames, passwords, and banking details.
  • Banking Fraud: Unauthorized access to bank accounts using stolen credentials or fake banking apps.
  • Credit Card Fraud: Illegal use of debit or credit card information for unauthorized purchases.
  • Online Shopping Fraud: Fake e-commerce websites that collect payments without delivering products.
  • Identity Theft: Stealing personal information to open accounts or commit crimes in another person's name.
  • Investment Scams: Fake investment schemes promising unrealistic profits.
  • Job Scams: Fraudsters offer fake jobs and demand registration or processing fees.
  • Lottery and Prize Scams: Victims receive fake messages claiming they have won prizes and are asked to pay fees.
  • Romance Scams: Criminals build online relationships to gain trust before asking for money.

Techniques Used by Cyber Fraudsters

  • Social Engineering
  • Phishing Emails
  • SMS Phishing (Smishing)
  • Voice Phishing (Vishing)
  • Fake Mobile Applications
  • Fake Customer Care Numbers
  • Malicious Links and Attachments
  • QR Code Scams
  • Password Guessing and Credential Theft
  • Fake Social Media Profiles

Case Studies

Case Study 1: Banking OTP Scam

A victim received a phone call from someone pretending to be a bank employee. The fraudster convinced the victim to share a One-Time Password (OTP). Using this OTP, the attacker transferred money from the victim's bank account.

Case Study 2: Fake Online Shopping Website

A customer purchased an expensive smartphone from a fake shopping website offering huge discounts. After payment, the website disappeared, and the customer never received the product.

Case Study 3: Phishing Email Attack

An employee received an email that appeared to be from the company's IT department requesting a password reset. After entering login credentials on a fake webpage, attackers gained access to the company's network.

How to Stay Safe

  • Never share OTPs or passwords.
  • Verify website URLs before making payments.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
  • Download apps only from official app stores.
  • Do not click suspicious links or attachments.
  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Regularly monitor bank transactions.
  • Keep your devices and antivirus software updated.
