Cyber Crimes in Everyday Life

Introduction

Cyber crimes in a smaller, everyday context are illegal activities that directly affect ordinary people in their personal, school, or small business life through computers and the internet. They usually involve stealing money or data, tricking people online, or misusing personal information rather than attacking big governments or critical infrastructure.

Simple Definition

Cyber crime at this small scale means any crime where a phone, laptop, or online service like email, social media, or banking apps is used to cheat, steal, threaten, or harass someone. The device can be the main target (hacking your account) or just the tool used to commit the crime (sending fake payment links).

Common Examples of Cyber Crimes

  • Phishing Messages: Fake messages that ask for passwords, OTPs, or banking details by pretending to be banks, delivery services, or government agencies.
  • Online Payment Fraud: Fraud involving fake shopping websites, QR code scams, and UPI or card payment frauds.
  • Social Media Hacking: Unauthorized access to social media accounts, impersonation, and cyberbullying through fake profiles or abusive messages.
  • Identity Theft: Using stolen personal data to open accounts or make purchases in another person's name.

Types of Cyber Crimes

  • Hacking: Gaining unauthorized access to computers, mobile phones, or online accounts.
  • Cyberbullying: Using digital platforms to threaten, insult, or harass other people.
  • Data Theft: Stealing personal, financial, or confidential information without permission.
  • Identity Theft: Misusing another person's personal information for unauthorized activities.
  • Online Shopping Scams: Creating fake shopping websites or advertisements to deceive customers.
  • Email Fraud: Sending misleading emails to collect sensitive information or trick people into making payments.
  • Social Media Impersonation: Creating fake profiles using another person's name or photographs.
  • Malware Attacks: Using harmful software to disrupt devices, steal information, or access data without permission.

Example of a Phishing Email

Cybercriminals often send fake emails pretending to be government departments or trusted organizations. These emails may contain suspicious verification links that attempt to collect personal information and account details.

Figure 3: Example of a fraudulent email used in phishing attacks.

How Cyber Crimes Affect Individuals

  • Financial loss due to online scams and fraud.
  • Loss of personal and confidential information.
  • Unauthorized access to social media and email accounts.
  • Misuse of personal photographs and information.
  • Privacy problems and emotional stress.
  • Loss of trust in online services and digital payments.

Case Studies

Case Study 1: Fake Bank Message

A person received a message claiming that their bank account would be blocked unless they updated their KYC details. The message contained a fake link. After entering personal and banking information, the person's details were misused by cybercriminals.

Case Study 2: Social Media Account Hacking

A student received a suspicious link through social media. After accessing the fake page and entering login details, someone gained unauthorized access to the student's account and sent misleading messages to friends.

Case Study 3: Online Shopping Fraud

A customer ordered a product from an unknown website advertising huge discounts. After making the payment, the product was never delivered, and the website stopped responding to the customer.

Prevention of Cyber Crimes

  • Use strong and unique passwords for online accounts.
  • Never share OTPs, passwords, or banking details.
  • Avoid clicking unknown links and suspicious messages.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).
  • Use trusted websites for online shopping and payments.
  • Keep mobile phones, computers, and applications updated.
  • Do not accept unknown friend requests or suspicious profiles.
  • Report cybercrime incidents to the appropriate authorities.

Conclusion

Cyber crimes are increasing in everyday digital life and can affect anyone who uses the internet. Awareness, responsible internet usage, strong passwords, and safe online practices can help protect individuals from cyber threats. Everyone should stay alert and report suspicious online activities to prevent cybercrime.
